# Pectin breakdown

Pectin holds plant cell walls together. As it weakens, the cells come apart and
a vegetable or bean turns tender.[^bassett][^atk]

## Levers

- **Water:** water has to get through a bean's seed coat and soften the cell
    walls inside before the bean turns tender.[^bassett]
- **Salt:** sodium takes the place of some of the calcium and magnesium holding
    a bean skin's pectin together, which weakens it.[^atk]
- **Temperature:** in fat at 82-107ºC (180-225ºF), vegetables soften without
    losing their structure. Fat that gets too hot fries them.[^allclad]

## Methods

### Confit

Confit cooks food submerged in fat, low and slow, until tender.[^allclad]

[Confit cabbage](../recipes/vegetables/confit-cabbage.md) sears its cut faces
first, because fat held that low never browns them.

### Brining beans

Salt in the soaking water softens bean skins, and brined beans burst less as
they cook.[^atk]

[Beans](../recipes/legumes/beans/index.md) soak in salted water to soften their
skins.
