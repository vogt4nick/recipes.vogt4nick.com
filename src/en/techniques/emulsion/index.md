# Emulsion

Oil and water separate unless something sits between them. In egg yolk, the
low-density lipoproteins break apart at the surface of each oil droplet and
their cholesterol and phospholipids settle there.[^mine] In mustard, it's
mucilage, a mix of proteins and polysaccharides around the seed hull.[^atk] In a
sauce of grated cheese and water, starch keeps the cheese from
clumping.[^bartolucci]

[^mine]: Mine, Y.
    ["Adsorption Behavior of Egg Yolk Low-Density Lipoproteins in Oil-in-Water Emulsions."](https://doi.org/10.1021/jf970306y)
    _Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry_ 46 (1): 36-41. 1998.

[^atk]: ["What Kind of Mustard Should Be Used in a Vinaigrette?"](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6627-what-kind-of-mustard-should-be-used-in-a-vinaigrette)
