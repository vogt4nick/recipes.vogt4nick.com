# Texture

The mouth judges crispness partly by ear. A crisp that sounds louder, or
brighter in the high frequencies, seems crisper and fresher, though the eater
rarely notices the sound doing it.[^zampini] Bubbles reach the tongue's sour
cells, where an enzyme turns dissolved carbon dioxide into acid.[^chandrashekar]

## Levers

- **Sound:** raising the overall loudness of a bite, or only its 2-20 kHz range,
    makes a potato chip seem crisper and fresher.[^zampini]
- **Carbonation:** carbonic anhydrase 4 on the surface of sour-sensing cells
    makes protons from carbon dioxide, and those cells are the sensor. Carbon
    dioxide still doesn't simply taste sour.[^chandrashekar]
