# Umami synergy

Glutamate on its own tastes mildly savory. Add inosinate or guanylate and the
umami multiplies, about eightfold in human tasters.[^kurihara]

Both bind T1R1/T1R3, the only umami receptor that shows the synergy.[^kurihara]
Glutamate sits deep in the hinge of a flytrap-shaped domain on T1R1. The
nucleotide binds near the opening and holds the trap closed, the shape that
keeps the receptor switched on.[^zhang][^mouritsen]

## Levers

- **Glutamate:** besides MSG itself, kombu, tomato, and cheese are rich in
    it.[^kurihara] A cheese gains free glutamate as it ages, because enzymes
    break its milk protein down into amino acids.[^uic-cheese]
- **Inosinate:** forms as ATP breaks down in meat and fish after slaughter, and
    in yellowtail it peaks about 10 hours after killing.[^kurihara]
- **Guanylate:** raw mushrooms have little, and dried shiitake have a
    lot.[^kurihara]
