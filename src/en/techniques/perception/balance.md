# Balance

Tastes in a mixture change each other. Salt suppresses bitterness more than it
suppresses sweetness, so salt in something bitter lets the sweetness
through.[^breslin] Sugar and acid each hold the other back.[^schifferstein]

## Levers

- **[Salt](../agents/salt.md):** a sodium salt with little salty taste of its
    own makes a bitter-sweet mixture taste sweeter, and leaves plain sugar water
    unchanged. The gain comes from muting the bitterness.[^breslin]
- **[Sugar](../agents/sugar.md) and [acid](../agents/acid.md):** citric acid
    suppresses the sweetness of sugar, and sugar suppresses the acid's sourness.
    How much sweetness is lost depends only on the acid, while the sourness lost
    depends on both.[^schifferstein]

## Recipes

[Coffee chess pie](../../recipes/desserts/pies/coffee-chess-pie.md) salts a
filling made bitter with espresso.

[Shrub](../../recipes/beverages/shrub.md) adds vinegar to its syrup a little at
a time and is tasted as it goes, because each addition lowers the sweetness as
it raises the sourness.
