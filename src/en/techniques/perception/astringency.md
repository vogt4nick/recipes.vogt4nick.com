# Astringency

Tannins, acids, and some metal salts bind the proteins in saliva and strip the
mouth of its lubrication. Pain and touch receptors read the friction as a dry,
puckering roughness, and whether those are separate sensations or one is still
debated.[^wang]

## Levers

- **Tannins:** the polyphenols in [tea](../agents/tea.md) and red wine bind
    saliva's proline-rich proteins into soluble complexes, which grow into a
    precipitate.[^wang]
- **[Acid](../agents/acid.md):** a lower pH thins saliva.[^wang]
- **Repetition:** sip after sip, the coating of salivary protein doesn't
    replenish fast enough, and the astringency lasts longer.[^wang]
- **Milk:** casein binds tea polyphenols, which leaves fewer of them to bind the
    saliva.[^piyasena]
- **Brewing temperature:** green tea brewed at 100ºC (212ºF) is more astringent
    than tea brewed at 70ºC (158ºF), because more catechins leach out.[^deng]
- **[Fat](../agents/fat.md):** fat and astringency work against each other. Tea,
    wine, and pickles eaten with fatty food make it seem less fatty, and the fat
    softens their astringency.[^peyrot]

    high-proof spirit pulls astringent compounds a weaker one leaves behind.
