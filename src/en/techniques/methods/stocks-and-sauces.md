# Stocks and sauces

A sauce gets its body from gelatin, starch, or an emulsion. Much of its flavor
is dissolved out of bones or the bottom of a pan.

## Stock

Stock is a decoction whose main product is gelatin, formed as hot water
hydrolyzes collagen.[^nist] Smaller pieces, a lower pH, and longer, hotter
cooking all raise the yield.[^gelatin] It's
[extraction](../mechanics/extraction.md) with water as the solvent.

## Deglazing

The browned bits stuck to a pan after searing, the fond, are concentrated
flavor, and hot liquid [dissolves](../mechanics/extraction.md) them. Water,
stock, wine, and vinegar all work.[^falkowitz]

[Braised beef](../../recipes/meats/beef/braised-beef.md) braises in the dutch
oven it seared in, so the stock takes up the fond.

## Roux

Flour cooked in fat thickens a sauce by
[gelatinization](../mechanics/gelatinization.md), and the longer it cooks, the
less it thickens. Against a white roux cooked 1 minute, a blond roux cooked 3
minutes has 14% less thickening power, and a peanut butter-colored roux cooked 5
minutes has 26% less. The darker roux tastes stronger.[^atk-roux]

## Slurry

Starch stirred into an equal amount of water and drizzled into a sauce thickens
it without powdery lumps. Starch settles out of the water quickly, so re-stir
the slurry right before it goes in.[^leung]

Hot bacon vinaigrette uses a slurry alongside
[mustard emulsification](#mustard-emulsification).

## Egg emulsification

As oil or butter is whisked into egg yolk a little at a time, the yolk's
low-density lipoproteins come apart at the surface of each droplet, leaving
cholesterol and phospholipids coating it.[^mine] That coating keeps the droplets
from running back together, which is
[emulsification](../mechanics/emulsification.md).

[Mayonnaise](../../recipes/sauces/hollandaise/mayonnaise/index.md) starts with
teaspoons of oil until the sauce thickens.

## Mustard emulsification

Mustard whisked into a vinaigrette holds the oil and vinegar together with the
mucilage around its seed hulls.[^atk-mustard]

## Starch emulsification

In a sauce of grated cheese and water, starch concentration is the key factor in
stability. Too little, and the sauce seizes into clumps, the "Mozzarella
phase."[^bartolucci]

## Mounting with butter

Butter whisked into a little warm liquid emulsifies. Keep the sauce below a
simmer; if bubbles form, pull it off the heat before the emulsion splits.[^fdl]

    Gives working numbers for extracting gelatin from bone in one short acid
