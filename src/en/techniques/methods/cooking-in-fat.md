# Cooking in fat

Fat carries heat to the food at whatever temperature the cook holds it. Hot
enough, it browns food in the
[Maillard reaction](../mechanics/maillard-reaction.md). Held low, it softens
food without browning it.

## Searing

Searing builds a crust on a surface dry enough to brown, in a pan hot enough to
drive off any water quickly.[^aoki][^earl]

A sauce browns the same way. Soy sauce poured down the hot side of a wok browns
as its water boils off against the metal; poured into the food, it sits among
wet ingredients near 100ºC (212ºF), and the stir-fry loses its _wok hei_.[^lieu]

## Sautéing

Sautéing cooks food quickly in a small amount of fat over high heat, tossing or
flipping it in the pan as it cooks.[^gisslen] Oil shimmers at about 135ºC
(275ºF), hot enough for vegetables, and starts to smoke at about 204ºC (400ºF),
the cue for meat.[^atk]

## Pan-frying

Pan-frying cooks food in just enough oil to cover the bottom of the pan, at
about 170ºC (340ºF) for meat and 190ºC (375ºF) for vegetables.[^remley] The food
stays in place rather than being tossed, usually for longer and at lower heat
than a sauté.[^gisslen]

## Sweating

Sweating keeps the Maillard reaction from starting. Vegetables cook slowly in a
little fat over low heat until they soften and turn translucent.[^nyorani] Salt
draws their water out by [osmosis](../mechanics/diffusion.md),[^rothman] and a
lid keeps that water in the pan. While the pan stays wet, the vegetables can't
get much hotter than 100ºC (212ºF). Color in the pan means the heat is too high.

## Velveting

Meat for a stir-fry is marinated with water, seasoning, starch, and oil, then
par-cooked in oil or water before it meets the wok. The
[starch](../mechanics/gelatinization.md) coating holds the marinade in and
shields the meat from the wok's heat, so it comes out silky.[^leung-velveting]
Tapioca starch makes an even silkier marinade than cornstarch.[^leung]

No recipe velvets yet.
[Cashew chicken](../../recipes/meats/poultry/cashew-chicken.md) and
[sweet & spicy stir fry](../../recipes/meats/poultry/sweet-and-spicy-stir-fry.md)
marinate in soy sauce without starch.

## Confit

Confit cooks food submerged in fat, low and slow, until tender.[^allclad] At
82-107ºC (180-225ºF), the fat softens vegetables by
[pectin breakdown](../mechanics/pectin-breakdown.md) without frying
them.[^allclad]

[Confit cabbage](../../recipes/vegetables/confit-cabbage.md) sears its cut faces
first, because fat held that low never browns them.
