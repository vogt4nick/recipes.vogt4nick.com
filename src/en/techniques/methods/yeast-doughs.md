# Yeast doughs

A yeast dough needs gas and something to hold it. Yeast makes the gas by
[fermentation](../mechanics/fermentation.md),[^redstar] and
[gluten](../mechanics/gluten-development.md) traps it.[^kasalt] Each step of
making bread works on one or both.

## Keeping a starter

A starter is a culture of wild yeast and bacteria kept alive on flour and water.
Commercial yeast is more vigorous than wild yeast, and even a little of it takes
over the culture.[^forkish]

[Starter](../../recipes/breads/leaven-breads/starter.md) never takes commercial
yeast for that reason. It keeps a month in the refrigerator, where the cold
slows it down.[^leo]

## Kneading

Kneading builds gluten fast, and bread dough is hard to
over-knead.[^anastopoulo] To check it, stretch a small piece of dough thin.
Strong gluten stretches thin enough to see light through before it tears.
Butter, sugar, and egg weaken a dough, so enriched doughs knead longer.[^hamel]

## Autolyse and folding

Water and time develop gluten without kneading.[^anastopoulo] An autolyse rests
the flour and water before the salt and leaven go in. During bulk fermentation,
folds stretch the dough every so often, and sourdough gets much of its strength
this way.[^hamel]

[Whole wheat](../../recipes/breads/leaven-breads/whole-wheat.md) does both. It
autolyses half an hour before mixing and folds through bulk fermentation, and
never kneads.

## Bulk fermentation and proofing

The dough's temperature at the end of mixing sets how fast it rises, so bakers
target one, often 24-25ºC (75-78ºF).[^leo] Warm dough sours mildly, with lactic
acid. Cooler dough leans acetic, and an overnight proof in the refrigerator
gives bread more depth of flavor.[^leo]

[Whole wheat](../../recipes/breads/leaven-breads/whole-wheat.md) checks its
dough temperature after mixing, and its optional night in the refrigerator
trades a day of waiting for that flavor.
