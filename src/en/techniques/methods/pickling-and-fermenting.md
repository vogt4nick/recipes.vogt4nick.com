# Pickling and fermenting

A pickle keeps because its acid inhibits microorganisms.[^uga] The acid can be
poured in as vinegar or made in place by bacteria, by
[fermentation](../mechanics/fermentation.md).[^mccord] Either way it seasons the
food from the outside in, by [diffusion](../mechanics/diffusion.md), and slowly.
Red wine penetrates less than 1 mm into beef short ribs in 18
hours.[^atk-marinade]

## Pickling

A pickle's acidity governs its safety as well as its taste and texture. The acid
inhibits microorganisms and denatures the enzymes that cause spoilage.[^uga]

[Pickled ginger](../../recipes/seasonings/pickles/pickled-ginger.md) is salted
for an hour before it goes into the vinegar, so the salt has pulled some of its
water out first.

## Culturing

Lactic acid bacteria in cream turn its lactose into lactic acid. The acid breaks
up the bundled casein proteins and rejoins them into a patchwork that holds the
fat and water in place, so the cream sours and thickens.[^mccord]

[Crème fraîche](../../recipes/sauces/creme-fraiche.md) takes its bacteria from
cultured buttermilk.

## Brewing

Yeast in a sweet liquid gives off carbon dioxide and alcohol.[^redstar] Cold
makes the yeast clump together and sink, which clears the drink. Wait until
fermentation is complete before chilling it.[^beersmith]

[Kvass](../../recipes/beverages/kvass.md) chills two days so the yeast settles
and the clear kvass can be poured off it.

## Marinating

Because a marinade moves so slowly into meat, it works on the surface, and it
matters most on small or thin pieces. An acidic marinade weakens collagen where
it reaches, and acid left too long turns the outermost layer mushy, not
tender.[^atk-marinade]

## Macerating

Sugar draws water out of fruit without any added liquid. Because it works more
slowly than salt, cooks use a heavy dose.[^macerate]

[Shrub](../../recipes/beverages/shrub.md) macerates fruit in sugar until the
juice it draws out dissolves the sugar into a syrup.
