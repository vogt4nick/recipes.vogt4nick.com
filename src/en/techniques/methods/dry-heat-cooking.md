# Dry-heat cooking

Dry-heat methods carry heat to the food without moisture.[^gisslen] Once a
surface dries, it can climb past 100ºC (212ºF) and brown in the
[Maillard reaction](../mechanics/maillard-reaction.md).[^aoki]

## Roasting

Roasting depends on dry heat reaching every piece so its surface can dry out and
brown.[^mcneill]

## Toasting

Toasting browns dry food, like nuts, seeds, spices, grains, and bread, in dry
heat or a little fat. Whole spices hold more of their oils than ground ones, so
they toast better,[^miller] and are ground after toasting.[^card] Burnt spices
taste harsh and bitter.[^miller]

## Resting

Food keeps cooking after it leaves the heat, and its proteins keep
[denaturing](../mechanics/denaturation.md). A roast leg of lamb rises an average
of 13ºC (23ºF) while resting, and its juices thicken as it cools; carved too
early, the juices run onto the board.[^thermapen] Bread keeps setting as it
cools; until then, baker Paul Adams says, it "is effectively undercooked," with
a sticky interior.[^white]
