# Curing and brining

Salt on food draws water out by [osmosis](../mechanics/diffusion.md),[^rothman]
and given time, the salty juice is drawn back in.[^earl] Salt also changes
meat's muscle proteins so they hold on to more water through cooking.[^bomze]

## Dry-brining

Salt on the surface draws the juices inside out to meet it. The salt dissolves
in them, and that brine is eventually reabsorbed. Left uncovered overnight, the
skin dries out too, so it crisps.[^earl]

## Wet-brining

Because the salt in a brine is already dissolved, wet-brining skips the wait for
the meat to give up its juices and works faster.[^bomze] A brined lean cut comes
out juicier for having gained water on top of what it keeps.[^bomze] The water
it adds is plain tap water, so it dilutes the meat's flavor,[^earl] and it has
to cook off before the surface browns. Water-chilled chicken takes up less of
the salt than air-chilled chicken, ending up drier.[^bomze]

[BBRRR chicken](../../recipes/meats/poultry/oven-roasted-chicken/bbrrr-chicken-breast.md)
brines boneless breasts, a lean cut that gains a lot from the extra water, then
pats them dry so the oven can brown them sooner.

## Curing

Curing preserves meat, fish, or olives with salt, sometimes mixed with sugar and
spices. The salt draws moisture out, leaving bacteria less to grow on, and works
into the food, where it makes conditions hostile to them.[^cooksinfo]

[Cured egg yolks](../../recipes/seasonings/cured-egg-yolk.md) sit 5-7 days in
salt and sugar, then dry in a low oven.

## Brining beans

Salt in the soaking water softens bean skins, and brined beans burst less as
they cook. Sodium takes the place of some of the calcium and magnesium holding a
bean skin's [pectin](../mechanics/pectin-breakdown.md) together.[^atk-beans]

[Beans](../../recipes/legumes/beans/index.md) soak in salted water to soften
their skins.
