# Gluten development

Wheat flour holds two proteins, glutenin and gliadin, that link into gluten once
water reaches them. Kneading or folding draws the gluten into long,
interconnected strands.[^anastopoulo] The stronger that web, the thinner it
stretches before it tears.[^hamel]

## Levers

- **Water:** more water forms more gluten, and too much makes a pie crust
    leathery.[^lopezalt] Gluten doesn't form in alcohol.[^lopezalt]
- **Fat:** fat coats the gluten-forming proteins, so little gluten forms once
    the liquid goes in. Gluten that has already formed stays strong, so a dough
    can be kneaded before its butter is added.[^exploratorium]
- **Salt:** salt tightens the gluten so the dough holds carbon dioxide
    better.[^kasalt]
- **Bran:** the bran in whole grain flour cuts the strands as they form.[^hamel]
- **Rest:** gluten relaxes as a dough rests.[^exploratorium]

## Methods

- [Kneading](../methods/yeast-doughs.md#kneading) -- work builds gluten fast.
- [Autolyse and folding](../methods/yeast-doughs.md#autolyse-and-folding) --
    water and time build it without kneading.
- [Resting](../methods/mixing-methods.md#resting-dough) -- relaxed gluten
    stretches instead of springing back.
- [Limiting gluten](../methods/mixing-methods.md#limiting-gluten) -- fat keeps
    pastry tender.
