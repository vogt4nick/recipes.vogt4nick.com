# Maillard reaction

A dry surface browns and builds new flavor in the Maillard reaction, which
happens around 150ºC (300ºF). Water on the surface holds it near 100ºC (212ºF)
while it boils off, so a wet surface steams and turns gray before it can
brown.[^aoki]

## Levers

- **Surface moisture:** the surface can't brown until its water is gone.[^aoki]
- **Crowding:** food releases moisture as it cooks, and pieces packed together
    trap it and steam.[^mcneill]
- **Temperature:** a pan for searing goes to 204-232ºC (400-450ºF) before the
    meat goes in.[^earl]
- **Time:** the longer pecans roast at 175ºC (347ºF), the more pyrazines, the
    nutty products of the reaction, they develop.[^gong]

## Methods

- [Searing](../methods/cooking-in-fat.md#searing) -- a hot pan dries a surface
    and browns it.
- [Sautéing](../methods/cooking-in-fat.md) and
    [pan-frying](../methods/cooking-in-fat.md#pan-frying) -- fat carries the
    pan's heat to the food.
- [Sweating](../methods/cooking-in-fat.md#sweating) -- a wet pan keeps the
    reaction from starting.
- [Roasting](../methods/dry-heat-cooking.md#roasting) -- dry air browns every
    surface it reaches.
- [Toasting](../methods/dry-heat-cooking.md#toasting) -- dry food browns fast.
- [Braising](../methods/combination-cooking.md#braising) -- the sear comes
    first, because the braise can't brown.
