# Fermentation

Yeast feeds on sugar and gives off carbon dioxide and alcohol.[^redstar] The gas
raises a dough or carbonates a drink. Lactic acid bacteria turn sugar into acid
instead, which sours a starter and thickens cultured cream.[^mccord]

## Levers

- **Temperature:** warmer dough ferments faster. Below about 2ºC (35ºF),
    fermentation nearly stalls.[^leo]
- **Salt:** about 2% of the flour's weight slows yeast without killing
    it.[^battilana] Leave it out and the yeast ferments too quickly.[^kasalt]
- **Hydration:** a wetter starter makes more lactic acid, and a stiffer one
    leans toward the sharper acetic acid.[^forkish]
- **Flour:** whole grain and rye flours ferment more vigorously than white
    flour.[^forkish]

## Methods

### Keeping a starter

A starter is a culture of wild yeast and bacteria kept alive on flour and water.
Commercial yeast is more vigorous than wild yeast, and even a little of it takes
over the culture.[^forkish]

[Starter](../recipes/breads/leaven-breads/starter.md) never takes commercial
yeast for that reason. It keeps a month in the refrigerator, where the cold
slows it down.[^leo]

### Bulk fermentation and proofing

The dough's temperature at the end of mixing sets how fast it rises, so bakers
target one, often 24-25ºC (75-78ºF).[^leo] Warm dough sours mildly, with lactic
acid. Cooler dough leans acetic, and an overnight proof in the refrigerator
gives bread more depth of flavor.[^leo]

[Whole wheat](../recipes/breads/leaven-breads/whole-wheat.md) checks its dough
temperature after mixing, and its optional night in the refrigerator trades a
day of waiting for that flavor.

### Culturing

Lactic acid bacteria in cream turn its lactose into lactic acid. The acid breaks
up the bundled casein proteins and rejoins them into a patchwork that holds the
fat and water in place, so the cream sours and thickens.[^mccord]

[Crème fraîche](../recipes/sauces/creme-fraiche.md) takes its bacteria from
cultured buttermilk.

### Brewing

Cold makes yeast clump together and sink, which clears a fermented drink. Wait
until fermentation is complete before chilling it.[^beersmith]

[Kvass](../recipes/beverages/kvass.md) chills two days so the yeast settles and
the clear kvass can be poured off it.
