# Sanshool

Red and green Sichuan peppercorns contain hydroxy-α-sanshool, the source of the
numbing, buzzing sensation Sichuan cooks call _má_.

- **[Chemesthesis](../mechanics/chemesthesis.md):** sanshool blocks two-pore
    potassium channels (KCNK3, KCNK9, and KCNK18) in touch neurons, so they fire
    with nothing touching them. It works without TRPV1 or TRPA1, the receptors
    behind chili and mustard.[^bautista] The tingle on the lips matches a
    vibration of about 50 Hz.[^hagura]

[^bautista]: Bautista, D. M., Y. M. Sigal, A. D. Milstein, J. L. Garrison, J. A.
    Zorn, P. R. Tsuruda, R. A. Nicoll, and D. Julius.
    ["Pungent Agents from Szechuan Peppers Excite Sensory Neurons by Inhibiting Two-Pore Potassium Channels."](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2143)
    _Nature Neuroscience_ 11 (7): 772-779. 2008.

[^hagura]: Hagura, N., H. Barber, and P. Haggard.
    ["Food Vibrations: Asian Spice Sets Lips Trembling."](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.1680)
    _Proceedings of the Royal Society B_ 280 (1770): 20131680. 2013.
