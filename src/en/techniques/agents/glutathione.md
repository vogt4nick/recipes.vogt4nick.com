# Glutathione

Garlic, onion, and meat contain glutathione, a tripeptide that makes other
flavors fuller without adding a taste of its own.

- **[Kokumi](../mechanics/kokumi.md):** glutathione activates the
    calcium-sensing receptor on taste cells, and blocking the receptor removes
    its effect.[^kuroda]
- **[Maillard reaction](../mechanics/maillard-reaction.md):** heated with
    glucose above 120ºC (248ºF), glutathione forms products that activate the
    receptor more strongly than glutathione itself. Heated alone, it breaks
    down.[^ahmad] See [roasting](../methods/dry-heat-cooking.md#roasting).

[^kuroda]: Kuroda, M., and N. Miyamura.
    ["Mechanism of the Perception of 'Kokumi' Substances and the Sensory Characteristics of the 'Kokumi' Peptide, γ-Glu-Val-Gly."](https://doi.org/10.1186/2044-7248-4-11)
    _Flavour_ 4: 11. 2015.

[^ahmad]: Ahmad, R., S. Deb-Choudhury, A. Subbaraj, and J. Kim.
    ["Activation of the Calcium-Sensing Receptor by Glutathione Maillard Products: Implications for Kokumi Sensation."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fochx.2025.102616)
    _Food Chemistry: X_ 29: 102616. 2025.

    Garlic, onion, and meat are named in the introduction as natural sources.
    The receptor results come from cell assays without a tasting panel.
