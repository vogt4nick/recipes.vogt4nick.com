# Caramelization

Sugar heated on its own breaks apart and forms new compounds that bring color,
flavor, and aroma.[^atk] Table sugar starts to caramelize around 171ºC (340ºF),
where it still tastes mild and very sweet.[^lam]

## Levers

- **Temperature:** the hotter it gets, the more bitter compounds form, and the
    caramel tastes less sweet. Most recipes stop at 182-191ºC (360-375ºF).[^lam]
- **Water:** a little water spreads the sugar evenly around the pan. Sugar
    heated dry can melt unevenly, and some of it burns.[^lam]
- **Crystals:** water evaporating at the edges of the pan leaves sugar crystals
    behind, and they seed more until the caramel turns grainy. Corn syrup or
    lemon juice in the sugar keeps them from forming, and so does leaving the
    syrup alone until it colors.[^lam]
- **Liquid:** caramel keeps cooking off the heat, and can still burn. Stirring
    in liquid or butter soaks up heat and slows it down.[^lam]

## Methods

### Dry caramel

Sugar melts without water. It's fast, and the sugar can scorch where it melts
first.[^lam]

[Nariyal burfi](../recipes/desserts/nariyal-burfi.md) melts its sugar in a
little butter and comes off the heat the moment it turns golden, because caramel
keeps darkening in a hot pan. The milk that goes in next stops it.

### Wet caramel

Sugar dissolves in water first, which melts it more evenly.[^lam] No recipe
makes a wet caramel yet.
