# Starch

Starch thickens a liquid as it heats in it, and too much heat undoes the work;
cornstarch cooked too long breaks down and thins the sauce again.[^leung] Heat
before the liquid goes in costs thickening too: browning flour in fat breaks its
starch chains, so a darker roux thickens less.[^atk] Starch also stabilizes. In
a sauce of grated cheese and water, starch concentration is the main factor that
keeps the cheese from clumping as the sauce heats.[^bartolucci]
