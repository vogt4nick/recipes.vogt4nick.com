# Diffusion

Water and what dissolves in it move from where they are concentrated toward
where they are not. Salt on food draws water out by osmosis,[^rothman] and given
time, the salty juice is drawn back in.[^earl]

## Levers

- **Concentration:** a brine left too long makes meat too salty.[^bomze] Tested
    pickle recipes call for vinegar of 5% acidity in the stated amount, because
    the acid has to reach a safe level.[^uga]
- **Distance:** red wine penetrates less than 1 mm into beef short ribs in 18
    hours.[^atk-marinade]
- **Temperature:** brown rice hydrates twice as fast at 50ºC (122ºF) as at 25ºC
    (77ºF).[^han-lim]
- **Time:** cooking time for soaked beans drops sharply within the first 3 hours
    of soaking and levels off after about 6.[^bassett]
- **Solute:** sugar draws water more slowly than salt, because sucrose stays a
    single molecule in solution while salt dissociates into two ions.[^macerate]

## Methods

### Dry-brining

Salt on the surface draws the juices inside out to meet it. The salt dissolves
in them, and that brine is eventually reabsorbed. Left uncovered overnight, the
skin dries out too, so it crisps.[^earl]

### Wet-brining

Because the salt in a brine is already dissolved, wet-brining skips the wait for
the meat to give up its juices and works faster.[^bomze] The water it adds is
plain tap water, so it dilutes the meat's flavor,[^earl] and it has to cook off
before the surface browns. Water-chilled chicken takes up less of the salt than
air-chilled chicken, ending up drier.[^bomze]

[BBRRR chicken](../recipes/meats/poultry/oven-roasted-chicken/bbrrr-chicken-breast.md)
brines boneless breasts, a lean cut that gains a lot from the extra water, then
pats them dry so the oven can brown them sooner.

### Curing

Curing preserves meat, fish, or olives with salt, sometimes mixed with sugar and
spices. The salt draws moisture out, leaving bacteria less to grow on, and works
into the food, where it makes conditions hostile to them.[^cooksinfo]

[Cured egg yolks](../recipes/seasonings/cured-egg-yolk.md) sit 5-7 days in salt
and sugar, then dry in a low oven.

### Marinating

Because a marinade moves so slowly into meat, it works on the surface, and it
matters most on small or thin pieces. An acidic marinade weakens collagen where
it reaches, and acid left too long turns the outermost layer mushy, not
tender.[^atk-marinade]

### Pickling

A pickle's acidity governs its safety as well as its taste and texture. The acid
inhibits microorganisms and denatures the enzymes that cause spoilage.[^uga]

[Pickled ginger](../recipes/seasonings/pickles/pickled-ginger.md) is salted for
an hour before it goes into the vinegar, so the salt has pulled some of its
water out first.

### Macerating

Sugar draws water out of fruit without any added liquid. Because it works more
slowly than salt, cooks use a heavy dose.[^macerate]

[Shrub](../recipes/beverages/shrub.md) macerates fruit in sugar until the juice
it draws out dissolves the sugar into a syrup.

### Soaking grains

White rice finishes absorbing water in about 30 minutes at 23ºC (73ºF), and a
longer soak only makes it cook up sticky.[^kim] Soaked rice cooks more evenly,
but even a 30-minute soak gives it a faint sewer-like off-flavor, most
noticeably in basmati.[^champagne] Bran slows the water down. Brown rice needs 2
hours at 25ºC (77ºF) to reach 20% moisture.[^han-lim]

Quinoa's bitterness comes from saponins in the outer layer of its seed coat.
Saponins are soap-like and dissolve in water, and warm water removes more of
them. Thirty minutes in water at 50ºC (122ºF) removes about 93%. The study gives
no figure for room temperature.[^el-hazzam]

[Basmati rice](../recipes/grains/rice/basmati-rice/index.md) stops soaking
before the grains turn sticky. The water poured off
[quinoa](../recipes/grains/quinoa/index.md) takes the saponins with it, and
[wheat berries](../recipes/grains/wheat-berries.md), still in their bran, need
the whole night.

### Soaking legumes

Water gets into a dry bean slowly, through its seed coat, and then has to soften
the cell walls inside before the bean turns tender.[^bassett]

Soaking also draws out some of the oligosaccharides that cause flatulence, and
pouring off the water gets rid of them. Of the four legumes tested, only lentils
lose more when cooked afterward.[^han-baik]

Soaking does not make beans safe to eat. Red kidney beans contain a toxic
lectin, phytohaemagglutinin, and four or five raw beans are enough to cause
vomiting. Most poisonings come from raw beans that were soaked. Ten minutes at a
boil destroys the lectin, and the FDA advises 30 to be safe. A slow cooker can
top out at 75ºC (167ºF), which leaves it intact.[^fda]

[Beans](../recipes/legumes/beans/index.md) pour off their soaking water along
with the sugars it drew out.

    quarts of cold water for an hour. Salting the same bird takes 6-24 hours.

[^han-lim]: Han, J.-A., and S.-T. Lim.
    ["Effect of Presoaking on Textural, Thermal, and Digestive Properties of Cooked Brown Rice."](https://doi.org/10.1094/CCHEM-86-1-0100)
    _Cereal Chemistry_ 86 (1): 100-105. 2009.

    Soaks japonica brown rice at 25ºC and 50ºC, then cooks it. Warmer water
    hydrates the rice twice as fast but leaches more starch. The paper covers
    brown rice only, not wheat berries or farro.

[^bassett]: Bassett, A., S. Hooper, and K. Cichy.
    ["Genetic Variability of Cooking Time in Dry Beans (_Phaseolus vulgaris_ L.) Related to Seed Coat Thickness and the Cotyledon Cell Wall."](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2020.109886)
    _Food Research International_ 141: 109886. 2021.

    Cooks fast- and slow-cooking bean lines unsoaked and after 3-24 hours of
    soaking, then looks at their seed coats and cell walls. Only common beans
    (_Phaseolus vulgaris_) are tested.

[^macerate]: ["Macerating vs. Salting."](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/9311-macerating-vs-salting)
    _America's Test Kitchen._

    Explains in a paragraph why sugar draws water more slowly than salt, and why
    cooks make up for it with a heavy dose.

[^cooksinfo]: ["Curing."](https://www.cooksinfo.com/curing) _CooksInfo._ 17 June
    2020\.

[^kim]: Kim, M.-H.
    ["Effect of Soaking Conditions on Texture of Cooked Rice."](https://www.koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO199203041987261.page)
    _Korean Journal of Food Science and Technology_ 24 (5): 511-514. 1992.

    Soaks Akibare, a japonica rice, in cold and room-temperature water and
    measures the texture of the cooked rice. Soaks of 10-20 minutes at room
    temperature give acceptable texture.

[^champagne]: Champagne, E. T., K. L. Bett-Garber, J. L. Thomson, F. F. Shih, J.
    Lea, and K. Daigle.
    ["Impact of Presoaking on Flavor of Cooked Rice."](https://doi.org/10.1094/CCHEM-85-5-0706)
    _Cereal Chemistry_ 85 (5): 706-710. 2008.

    A USDA tasting panel compares 11 milled rices cooked with and without a
    30-minute soak. The paper takes even cooking as a given and measures only
    flavor. The off-flavor is strongest in its two basmati samples and one U.S.
    long grain.

[^el-hazzam]: El Hazzam, K., M. Mhada, M. L. Metougui, K. El Kacimi, M. Sobeh,
    M. Taourirte, and A. Yasri.
    ["Box-Behnken Design: Wet Process Optimization for Saponins Removal From _Chenopodium quinoa_ Seeds and the Study of Its Effect on Nutritional Properties."](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2022.906592)
    _Frontiers in Nutrition_ 9: 906592. 2022.

    A lab study that tunes quinoa washing to remove as many saponins as
    possible. An early screening from 20ºC to 60ºC finds that warmer water
    removes more, but the paper reports numbers only from 50ºC up. The 93% run
    uses 7 mL of water per gram of quinoa. The paper also notes that saponins
    can be toxic at high concentrations.

[^han-baik]: Han, I. H., and B.-K. Baik.
    ["Oligosaccharide Content and Composition of Legumes and Their Reduction by Soaking, Cooking, Ultrasound, and High Hydrostatic Pressure."](https://doi.org/10.1094/CC-83-0428)
    _Cereal Chemistry_ 83 (4): 428-433. 2006.

    Soaks lentils, chickpeas, peas, and soybeans for 3 or 12 hours and then
    cooks them. Chickpeas start with about twice the oligosaccharides of yellow
    peas.

[^fda]: U.S. Food and Drug Administration.
    ["Phytohaemagglutinin (Kidney Bean Lectin)."](https://www.fda.gov/media/83271/download)
    _Bad Bug Book: Handbook of Foodborne Pathogenic Microorganisms and
    Natural Toxins._ 2nd ed. 2012.

    The FDA's food-safety handbook. Cooking takes red kidney beans from
    20,000-70,000 hemagglutinating units down to 200-400. White kidney beans
    have about a third as much lectin as red. The 10-minute figure comes from a
    British study.
