# Brining

Salt changes meat's muscle proteins so they hold on to more water through
cooking. Brining gets salt into raw meat well ahead of time, rubbed on dry or
dissolved in water.[^bomze]

## Dry-brining

Salt on the surface draws the juices inside out to meet it. The salt dissolves
in them, and that brine is eventually reabsorbed. Left uncovered overnight, the
skin dries out too, so it crisps.[^earl]

## Wet-brining

Because the salt in a brine is already dissolved, wet-brining skips the wait for
the meat to give up its juices and works faster. A brined lean cut comes out
juicier for having gained water on top of what it keeps.[^bomze]

That water is plain tap water, so it dilutes the meat's flavor.[^earl] It also
has to cook off before the surface browns. A brine left too long makes the meat
too salty, and water-chilled chicken takes up less of the salt than air-chilled
chicken, ending up drier.[^bomze]

[BBRRR chicken](../../recipes/meats/poultry/oven-roasted-chicken/bbrrr-chicken-breast.md)
brines boneless breasts, a lean cut that gains a lot from the extra water, then
pats them dry so the oven can brown them sooner.
